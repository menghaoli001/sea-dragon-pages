GMU Team research briefing

Network Structure and Vulnerability
in the Sea Dragon Case

Study Purpose

Using court filings from Sea Dragon and related proceedings, we examine whether recorded relationships depend on a few people, companies or financial accounts. The aim is to identify structural dependence and assess how strongly the findings depend on the available evidence.

A node is a recorded person, organization, account or address. A link represents a relationship described in a filing. Two nodes are a reachable pair if at least one path joins them, directly or through other nodes.

Methods

  • Simulation groups: People, companies described as shells, bank accounts and cryptocurrency records. Exchange accounts and addresses are also compared separately.
  • Simulation procedures: Remove up to ten nodes from one group, selected randomly, by most direct links (degree), or by most connecting paths (betweenness). Main rankings are set before removal; random selection uses 1,000 runs.
  • Performance: Measure the percentage of originally reachable pairs still connected. Count each pair once. Keep the original denominator, including pairs lost when a node is deleted. Higher retention means greater structural resilience; lower retention means greater simulated disruption.
  • Scope: Delete only the selected node and its links. No automatic account closures, replacement or adaptation. Equal numbers removed do not imply equal costs or practical feasibility.

Data Preparation

How are court filings converted into network data?

Data Extraction and Validation Workflow

Court filings are converted to page-aware text, with OCR where needed. Two models extract candidate data; a third reviews disagreements against the source. Rule-based checks and evidence review remain separate steps.

Agreement between models or a quotation match does not establish that a statement is true. Unresolved records retain their review flags.

How these tables are used: node IDs connect the two tables. Each included relationship is treated as an undirected, unweighted link, and repeated evidence for the same pair does not create additional links. Degree and betweenness are calculated from the starting network to select eligible nodes. Removing a node also removes every link attached to it.

Network inclusion and removal eligibility are separate decisions. Rows marked “No” are excluded during preparation, along with links attached to those nodes. Cards, named banks and unclear bank references are retained in both networks but excluded from main targeting. A retained node can still connect other nodes even when it cannot be selected in a main simulation group. A link marked “No” is omitted from that network before the simulation begins. Actual relationship records must retain their supporting claims and source references for checking.

Simulation Example: Removing One Account

This hypothetical chain has 5 nodes and 4 direct links, but all ten possible pairs are reachable. Its density is 4 ÷ 10 = 40%; its starting reachable-pair retention is 100%. A pair counts once, even when several paths connect it.

Starting network

5 nodes · 4 direct links · 10 reachable pairs

Scenario A: remove the middle account X

Two separate groups remain, each containing one reachable pair. Retention is 2 ÷ 10 = 20%.

4 remaining nodes · 2 direct links · 2 reachable pairs

Scenario B: remove the end account Y

Restart from the original network. The four remaining nodes stay connected, retaining six pairs: 6 ÷ 10 = 60%. Both scenarios use the same original denominator of ten; pairs involving a deleted node count as lost.

4 remaining nodes · 3 direct links · 6 reachable pairs

Compare the three selection methods

Only accounts X and Y are eligible in this example. Random selection gives each an equal chance. Degree selects the account with more direct links; betweenness selects the account that lies on more shortest paths between other nodes.

Selection method Account removed Reachable pairs retained
Random X or Y, each with 50% probability 40% average
Degree X: two direct links, compared with one for Y 20%
Betweenness X: connects the two sides of the chain 20%

The random average is (20% + 60%) ÷ 2 = 40%. Higher retention means greater structural resilience; lower retention means greater simulated disruption. Degree and betweenness select the same account here, but need not do so in a larger network.
